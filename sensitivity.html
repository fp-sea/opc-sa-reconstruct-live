How to read this page

The live daily gallery's Surface Analysis panel — what everyone actually sees — is produced by detect_fronts_tfp at its real, tuned-and-verified defaults: θe (theta-e) as the thermal field, an adaptive gradient-magnitude threshold (strict at the 98th percentile, relaxed to the 65th percentile within ~650km of a detected low), and wind-shift classification — a front's cold/warm/ stationary type is decided by the 850mb wind's own cross-front component, not by how fast the front itself is moving. The amber-bordered "Production baseline" panel below always shows exactly this, for whichever lead/model is selected, unaffected by any toggle on this page.

Every cell in the grid to its right, however, uses a DIFFERENT classification method — K3 front-speed classification, not wind-shift — so no grid cell, not even the 98th-percentile row, reproduces what production actually renders. The grid compares K3 against itself across percentile/speed-threshold combinations; the baseline panel is the fixed reference point, not a member of the grid.

grad_threshold_percentile (grid rows)
How selective the detector is about calling a temperature gradient sharp enough to plot as a front at all — higher = only the very strongest gradients qualify. Production's own strict threshold is 98 (relaxing to 65 near a low); the grid also sweeps 50, 65, 80, more permissive values the academic literature suggests as alternatives.
front_speed_threshold_ms (grid columns) — "K3"
The speed (m/s) a front's own gradient-magnitude ridge must clear, by this experimental measure, to be classified as moving (cold/warm) rather than stationary. Three real, independently-sourced anchors, not one guess: 1.5 m/s (the front-detection literature's own commonly-cited value), 3.0 m/s (production's own wind-shift threshold, reused here only as a convenient middle column for comparison — NOT the same physical quantity as K3), and 5.144 m/s (10 knots, the OPC Unified Surface Analysis Manual's own literal stationary-front definition).
Thermal field
θe (theta-e, equivalent potential temperature) is production's own choice. θw (theta-w, wet-bulb potential temperature) is the academic literature's more common choice for this method — the NWS manual specifically endorses θe for oceanic domains instead, so this is a genuinely open, unresolved tension, not settled by this page. See the reference guide for the physical distinction.
K1 gate
off matches production (no pre-filter). on adds an extra filter that drops the weakest 25% of the raw TFP field's own magnitude BEFORE the front line is even traced — a literature-suggested threshold. Real, counter-intuitive finding from testing this: it can occasionally INCREASE the final front count rather than reduce it, by fragmenting one continuous front into several shorter pieces that each independently re-qualify (see detect_fronts_experimental.py's own docstring for the measured numbers).
